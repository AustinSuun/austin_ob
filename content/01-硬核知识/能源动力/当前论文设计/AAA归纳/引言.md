# 1. Introduction

3D object detection from point clouds serves as a cornerstone for autonomous driving and robotics perception systems [?]. Unlike 2D images, which possess a regular grid structure, LiDAR point clouds are inherently sparse, unordered, and unstructured [?]. To process such data, existing approaches typically fall into two categories: voxel-based methods [?,?] which regularize points into grids but suffer from quantization loss, and point-based methods [?,?] which preserve geometric details but incur high computational costs in neighbor searching. Recently, Transformer-based architectures [?,?] have achieved remarkable success by modeling long-range dependencies. However, the quadratic complexity $O(N^2)$ of the self-attention mechanism hinders their scalability to large-scale point cloud scenarios containing hundreds of thousands of points.

To alleviate the computational burden, State Space Models (SSMs), particularly the recent Mamba architecture [?], have emerged as a promising alternative. Mamba offers linear complexity $O(N)$ regarding sequence length while maintaining a global receptive field. However, applying Mamba to 3D point clouds faces a fundamental challenge: **Serialization**. Since Mamba is designed for 1D causal sequences, 3D points must be flattened before processing. State-of-the-art methods [?,?] typically rely on fixed spatial orderings, such as Hilbert curves [?] or Z-order curves [?]. While these geometric strategies preserve spatial proximity to some extent, they are "blind" to the semantic content of the data. For instance, a fixed Hilbert curve treats a critical pedestrian point and a background ground point indistinguishably, forcing the model to process sequences that may lack semantic continuity. This mismatch between static geometric ordering and dynamic semantic structure leads to suboptimal information flow and hinders the model's ability to focus on foreground objects.

In this paper, we argue that the serialization of point clouds should be driven by **semantic importance** rather than mere spatial coordinates. Drawing inspiration from physical field theory, we propose a novel perspective: treating feature responses as "mass" and their interactions as "gravity." We introduce **Gravity-Mamba**, a physics-aware backbone that leverages gravitational principles to guide both sequence construction and structural completion.

Our approach comprises two core components designed to address the limitations of existing SSM-based and sparse detectors. First, to resolve the serialization bottleneck, we propose **Gravity Streamline Mapping (GSM)**. Instead of rigid spatial indexing, GSM constructs a feature-space gravity field where points with high feature magnitude (simulating large mass) exert stronger attraction. This mechanism dynamically organizes points into semantic "basins," ensuring that the Mamba engine prioritizes high-information regions—creating a "streamline" of features that flows from object cores to contexts. Second, to address the inherent sparsity and occlusion in LiDAR data (e.g., hollows in distant objects), we introduce the **Ray-Gravity Guided Expansion (RGGE)** module. By combining the physical properties of LiDAR rays with local geometric gravity, RGGE adaptively densifies sparse regions and fills internal voids, enhancing the structural integrity of object representations without introducing excessive noise.

To summarize, our main contributions are as follows:

- We propose **Gravity-Mamba**, the first 3D point cloud backbone that integrates physical gravity field theory with State Space Models, offering a fresh perspective on point cloud serialization and feature abstraction.
    
- We design **Gravity Streamline Mapping (GSM)**, a dynamic serialization strategy that replaces static spatial sorting with mass-based gravitational sorting, significantly improving the semantic coherence of input sequences for Mamba.
    
- We develop the **Ray-Gravity Guided Expansion (RGGE)** module, which effectively mitigates point sparsity by leveraging ray physics and geometric constraints to densify object structures.
    
- Extensive experiments on [?] and [?] datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance, validating the effectiveness of physics-inspired designs in 3D deep learning.

# 1. 引言 (Introduction)

基于点云的 3D 目标检测是自动驾驶和机器人感知系统的基石 [?]。与具有规则网格结构的 2D 图像不同，激光雷达（LiDAR）点云本质上是**稀疏（Sparse）**、**无序（Unordered）**且**非结构化（Unstructured）**的 [?]。为了处理此类数据，现有的方法通常分为两类：**基于体素（Voxel-based）**的方法 [?,?]，它们将点规范化为网格，但会遭受量化损失；以及**基于点（Point-based）**的方法 [?,?]，它们保留了几何细节，但在邻域搜索中会产生高昂的计算成本。近年来，基于 **Transformer** 的架构 [?,?] 通过对长程依赖关系的建模取得了显著成功。然而，自注意力机制（Self-attention）的**二次复杂度 $O(N^2)$** 阻碍了其在包含数十万个点的大规模点云场景中的扩展能力。

为了减轻计算负担，**状态空间模型（SSMs）**，特别是最近的 **Mamba 架构** [?]，已成为一种有前景的替代方案。Mamba 在保持全局感受野的同时，实现了关于序列长度的**线性复杂度 $O(N)$**。然而，将 Mamba 应用于 3D 点云面临一个根本性的挑战：**序列化（Serialization）**。由于 Mamba 是为 1D **因果序列**设计的，3D 点必须在处理前被展平。目前最先进的方法 [?,?] 通常依赖于固定的**空间排序**，如希尔伯特曲线（Hilbert curves）[?] 或 Z 序曲线（Z-order curves）[?]。虽然这些几何策略在一定程度上保留了空间邻近性，但它们对数据的**语义内容**是“盲目”的。例如，固定的希尔伯特曲线会无差别地处理关键的行人点和背景地面点，迫使模型处理缺乏语义连续性的序列。这种静态几何排序与动态语义结构之间的不匹配，导致了**次优的信息流**，并阻碍了模型聚焦于前景物体的能力。

在本文中，我们认为点云的序列化应由**语义重要性**驱动，而不仅仅是空间坐标。受物理场论的启发，我们提出了一个新的视角：将特征响应视为“质量”，将其相互作用视为“重力”。我们引入了 **Gravity-Mamba**，这是一种物理感知的骨干网络，利用重力原理来指导序列构建和结构补全。

我们的方法包含两个核心组件，旨在解决现有基于 SSM 和稀疏检测器的局限性。首先，为了解决序列化瓶颈，我们提出了 **重力流线映射 (Gravity Streamline Mapping, GSM)**。GSM 不采用僵硬的空间索引，而是构建一个特征空间重力场，其中具有高特征幅度（模拟大质量）的点施加更强的吸引力。该机制动态地将点组织成语义“流域（Basins）”，确保 Mamba 引擎优先处理高信息量区域——从而创建一条从物体核心流向背景上下文的特征“流线”。其次，为了解决 LiDAR 数据固有的稀疏性和遮挡问题（例如远距离物体的空洞），我们引入了 **射线重力引导的拓展 (Ray-Gravity Guided Expansion, RGGE)** 模块。通过结合激光雷达射线的物理特性与局部几何重力，RGGE 自适应地对稀疏区域进行稠密化并填充内部空洞，在不引入过多噪声的情况下增强了物体表示的结构完整性。

综上所述，我们的主要贡献如下：

- 我们提出了 **Gravity-Mamba**，这是首个将物理重力场理论与状态空间模型（SSMs）相结合的 3D 点云骨干网络，为点云序列化和特征抽象提供了全新的视角。
    
- 我们设计了 **重力流线映射 (GSM)**，这是一种动态序列化策略，用基于质量的重力排序取代了静态空间排序，显著提高了 Mamba 输入序列的语义连贯性。
    
- 我们开发了 **射线重力引导的拓展 (RGGE)** 模块，该模块利用射线物理和几何约束有效地缓解了点云稀疏性，实现了物体结构的稠密化。
    
- 在 [?] 和 [?] 数据集上的广泛实验表明，我们的方法达到了最先进（SOTA）的性能，验证了物理启发式设计在 3D 深度学习中的有效性。
